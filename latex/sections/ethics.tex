\section{Ethical Considerations}
\label{sec:ethics}

\paragraph{Exposure to harmful content.}
The datasets used in this study contain memes that may include hateful,
discriminatory, misogynistic, offensive, politically sensitive, or otherwise
disturbing material.
Researchers who inspect raw memes, retrieved or generated knowledge
candidates, rationales, or error cases may therefore be exposed to harmful
content.
We reduce unnecessary exposure by separating automated experiments from
manual inspection, limiting qualitative review to samples needed for
validation and analysis, and avoiding gratuitous reproduction of harmful
examples in the paper.
If human evaluation is conducted in future work, annotators should receive
an explicit content warning, be allowed to pause or withdraw, and not be
required to undergo repeated exposure to particularly severe material.

\paragraph{Dataset provenance and privacy.}
We use existing meme benchmarks for research on harmful-content
understanding while preserving their original sample identifiers, dataset
families, and domain metadata.
HarMeme is used only as the source collection for training and validation,
whereas FHM is reserved as a held-out target benchmark.
As described in Section~\ref{sec:setup:protocol}, FHM does not participate
in model training, prompt development, threshold selection,
retrieval-resource construction, or other model-selection decisions.

Memes may depict identifiable people or contain usernames, logos, public
figures, and contextual references.
Our task concerns interpretation of meme content rather than identification
of individuals.
The framework is not designed to infer private identity, unobserved
demographic characteristics, or other sensitive personal information
beyond the content-level categories explicitly defined by the task schema.
Any public release of code, manifests, or derived artifacts should respect
the distribution terms of the original datasets and should not redistribute
raw images when such redistribution is not permitted.

\paragraph{Annotation provenance and uncertainty.}
Original benchmark labels are retained for harmfulness.
The finer-grained structured fields---target presence, target granularity,
primary intent, rhetorical tactics, and multimodal relation---are stored
together with annotation provenance, validity masks, confidence or audit
metadata where available.
In particular, the structured FHM annotations used in this work are
agent-generated, schema-validated silver annotations rather than
human-adjudicated gold labels.
We therefore report them explicitly as silver structured evaluation and
provide field-level coverage and valid-sample counts.

Automated annotation can reproduce cultural assumptions and systematic
errors of the models used to produce it.
Moreover, categories such as communicative intent or rhetorical tactic may
admit more than one reasonable interpretation in ambiguous cases.
The annotation protocol therefore preserves unknown, ambiguous, or masked
states instead of forcing every field into a confident class.
We do not report these silver annotations as equivalent to independently
adjudicated human judgments.

\paragraph{External knowledge and contextual bias.}
Contextual information can help interpret implicit references but may also
be incomplete, weakly relevant, culturally specific, or factually
incorrect.
For this reason, retrieved items, generated hypotheses, fallback
candidates, verified knowledge, and rejected candidates remain
distinguishable through provenance.
The evidence-aware verifier evaluates relevance, validity-related cues, and
task-specific support before candidates are used by downstream reasoning;
this procedure does not guarantee factual correctness.

The paper-eligible retrieval resource is restricted to the predefined
HarMeme training-side corpus rather than live Web retrieval.
Nevertheless, the source data and generated contextual hypotheses may still
reflect dominant cultural, geographic, linguistic, or political
perspectives.
Such representational imbalance may lead to uneven performance across
communities or contexts.
We retain candidate provenance so that these failures can be inspected
rather than treating the acquired context as universally authoritative.

\paragraph{Rationale and evidence-attribution limitations.}
Generated rationales are intended to make model outputs easier to inspect,
not to serve as authoritative factual explanations.
A fluent rationale may omit relevant context, overstate weak evidence, or
introduce an unsupported claim.
Accordingly, rationale text is not used as the formal source of
harmfulness, target, intent, rhetorical-tactic, or multimodal-relation
predictions.
Structured metrics are computed from the corresponding prediction heads,
while rationale properties are evaluated separately using the diagnostics
described in Section~\ref{sec:analysis:evidence}.

Similarly, selected evidence, verifier records, and gating or attention
signals should not be interpreted as causal explanations of the model's
decision.
They are diagnostic records showing which information was available,
selected, filtered, or emphasized by the implemented pipeline.

\paragraph{Risks of automated moderation.}
False positives may incorrectly flag satire, criticism, political speech,
or benign humor, whereas false negatives may allow harmful material to
remain undetected.
Such errors may disproportionately affect communities whose linguistic or
cultural conventions are poorly represented in the source data.
The proposed framework should therefore not be deployed as an autonomous
moderation authority.
Its predictions, evidence records, and rationales are more appropriately
used as decision-support signals for trained human reviewers.

The structured outputs should also not be interpreted as judgments about a
person's private beliefs or intentions outside the particular meme being
analyzed.
Model confidence does not eliminate the need for contextual and human
review, especially in ambiguous, culturally dependent, or high-impact
cases.

\paragraph{Dual-use considerations.}
The same capabilities used to analyze harmful memes could be misused to
study weaknesses in moderation systems or to construct content intended to
evade detection.
To reduce unnecessary amplification of harmful material, released research
artifacts should emphasize model interfaces, evaluation protocols,
provenance-aware outputs, and aggregate measurements rather than
unnecessary reproduction of harmful examples.
Retrieved or generated harmful statements should likewise not be presented
as endorsed factual information.

\paragraph{Human evaluation and oversight.}
The current pipeline can export review packages for evidence relevance,
rationale grounding, verifier decisions, and stage-level error analysis,
but it does not itself provide human gold judgments.
No human-evaluation result is included in the empirical claims unless
independent annotations have been collected, validated, and checked for
agreement before the final result freeze.
Any future human study should use informed participation, clear annotation
guidelines, appropriate compensation, secure data handling, procedures for
resolving disagreement, and any institutional review required by the
relevant organization.
